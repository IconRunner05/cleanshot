# Owner acceptance pass

The hands-on check the owner runs on the reference PC with a release candidate before v1.0.0 is tagged (CHARTER 9, D-102). One step per feature: do what it says, and tick it only if you see what it says. Anything else is a failed step; note what you saw.

Start from a release candidate exe downloaded from its GitHub pre-release, with no `cleanshot.toml` next to it or in `%APPDATA%\Cleanshot` from an earlier run (C-05). Default hotkeys: PrtSc captures a region (Ctrl+Shift+4 if Windows' "Use the Print screen key to open screen capture" is on), Shift+PrtSc the last region, Ctrl+PrtSc the full screen, Ctrl+Shift+5 the capture panel.

## Start and tray

- [ ] F-53, F-50: Run `cleanshot.exe`. A Cleanshot icon appears in the tray and no window opens. Run it again: no second icon, and the region overlay opens.
- [ ] F-50: Right-click the tray icon. The menu shows Capture region, Capture screen, Settings and Exit. Double-click the icon: the region overlay opens.
- [ ] F-52: Turn on Settings > Accessibility > Keyboard > "Use the Print screen key to open screen capture" and restart Cleanshot. A tray balloon names that setting, and Ctrl+Shift+4 opens the overlay. Turn the setting off again and restart.

## Capture

- [ ] F-01, F-02: Press PrtSc. Every monitor dims at once, with no flicker of the overlay in the capture. Drag a rectangle: it stays bright, with an accent border, 8 handles and a `W x H` pill in physical pixels. Press Esc and PrtSc again, move the pointer over a window: it lights up by its frame. Click without moving: that window becomes the selection, with handles. Press Esc and PrtSc, then press and move one pixel before releasing: it is still a click, and picks the window (D-122).
- [ ] F-04: While dragging, a loupe shows the pixels under the pointer zoomed, with a grid, the coordinates and the colour's hex value. It goes when you let go.
- [ ] F-03: Drag a handle, drag inside the selection, and press the arrow keys (Shift for 10 px). The selection resizes, moves and nudges.
- [ ] F-05: A frosted toolbar sits near the bottom of the monitor (at the top if the selection is there), with Copy focused. Tab moves the focus; Esc closes the overlay and nothing is copied or saved.
- [ ] F-01 on mixed DPI: With two monitors at different scales, capture a region across both and paste it into Paint. It is sharp, at each monitor's physical pixels.
- [ ] F-01 dock and undock (D-151): Start Cleanshot with `--log` on the laptop screen alone, capture a region and paste it into Paint. Plug in an external monitor and capture a region on each screen: both paste in full, not black. Unplug it and capture again: the laptop screen pastes in full. The log's `capture copied=` lines all read `duplication`.
- [ ] F-20: Capture, press Enter, and paste into Outlook, Teams, Word, Paint and Edge. Each shows the capture.
- [ ] F-21: Capture and press Ctrl+S. A file `Screenshot <date> <time>.png` appears in Pictures\Screenshots. Save a second capture: both files are there, and neither replaced the other.
- [ ] F-22: Capture and press Ctrl+Shift+S. The Windows save dialog opens. Pick an existing file: it asks before replacing it.
- [ ] F-06: Press Shift+PrtSc. The last copied or saved rectangle is copied at once, with no overlay.
- [ ] F-07: Press Ctrl+PrtSc. The monitor under the pointer is captured.
- [ ] F-08, F-58: Open a few windows, minimise one, put one on another virtual desktop, and show a click-through overlay (for example a game's FPS counter). Capture, wait for the thumbnail, then press PrtSc and W. Move the pointer: each window lights up by its frame, without its shadow. The minimised window, the one on the other desktop and Cleanshot's own thumbnail never light up, the click-through overlay is passed over for the window under it, and bare desktop, the wallpaper, lights up the whole monitor. Click a window: it becomes the selection. Press W, Tab a few times and Enter: the window lit up is selected. Press Esc, then Alt+PrtSc: the overlay opens already picking a window, and a click selects one. Windows' own Alt+PrtSc copy of the active window does not happen while Cleanshot runs. Turn on Narrator and do it again: Narrator says "Window to capture" and the title of each window the highlight moves to, and "Capture screen" when the pointer rests on bare desktop.
- [ ] F-01, F-08 (D-122): Press PrtSc and click once on a window without dragging: that window becomes the selection, by its frame. Press PrtSc and drag: a rectangle is selected as before, even when the drag starts over a window. Press PrtSc and click once on bare desktop: the whole monitor under the pointer is selected.
- [ ] F-09: Capture and press Enter. A thumbnail slides in at the bottom-right of that monitor, and the window you were in keeps the focus. Hover it: it stays, with Save and Dismiss along its bottom. Move away: it fades after about 5 s. Capture again and drag the thumbnail into Word or Explorer: the image or a PNG file lands there. Capture again and click it: the editor opens with the capture.
- [ ] F-11: Click into Notepad and press Ctrl+Shift+5. The capture panel opens at the bottom of the monitor under the pointer, with Region chosen and the focus on Capture. Type: the keys go to the panel, not Notepad. Tab to Window and press Enter, then Tab to Timer and press Enter twice: it reads "5 s". Press Esc: the panel closes and Notepad has the focus again. Press Ctrl+Shift+5 again: Window and 5 s are still chosen. Click into Notepad while the panel shows: the panel stays; Ctrl+Shift+5 brings it back to the front.
- [ ] F-10: In the panel choose Screen and 3 s and press Capture. A pill reading "Capturing in 3" counts down where the panel was, and the focus stays where it was before the panel opened. Open the Start menu while it counts: the capture holds the open menu, and neither the panel nor the countdown. Start another and press Esc while it counts: nothing is captured. Choose Window and 3 s and press Capture: when the count ends, the overlay opens picking a window.
- [ ] F-11, F-58: Turn on Narrator and press Ctrl+Shift+5. Press Tab: Narrator names each control as the focus reaches it. Tab to Window and press Enter: Narrator says it is selected. Tab to Timer and press Enter: Narrator says "3 seconds". Press Esc, capture a region and press Tab: Narrator names each toolbar control as the focus moves.
- [ ] F-12 Options: Press Ctrl+Shift+5 and click Options: a menu opens beside it with Save folder..., PNG, JPG, BMP, Show pointer, Show a thumbnail and Remember last selection, PNG and Show a thumbnail checked. Click JPG, then capture a region and Save: the file is a .jpg, and Settings shows JPG. Select a region with PrtSc, press Shift+Tab to reach Options and Enter: the menu opens; arrow down and press Esc: the menu closes and the selection is still there; press Esc again: the overlay closes. Open Options, choose Save folder..., pick a folder and Save a capture: it lands there. Turn on Narrator: each item is read with its state.
- [ ] F-12 Show pointer: On the capture panel, open Options and click Show pointer. Hover a link in Edge so the pointer is a hand, press Ctrl+PrtSc and paste into Paint: the hand is in the image with its fingertip on the link, and the frozen screen showed it before the copy. Click Show pointer again and capture: no pointer.
- [ ] F-12 Remember last selection: On the capture panel, open Options and click Remember last selection. Press PrtSc, select a region and copy it. Press PrtSc again: the overlay opens with that region selected. Press it again from the capture panel with Region chosen: the same. With two monitors, select a region on the second, unplug it and press PrtSc: nothing is selected. Click Remember last selection again: PrtSc opens with nothing selected.
- [ ] F-13: Turn off Settings > Accessibility > Visual effects > Animation effects. The overlay appears with no fade. Turn on a high-contrast theme: the overlay and toolbar use its colours.

## Editor

- [ ] F-23: Capture a region about a quarter of the screen and press E. The overlay closes and the editor opens with the capture at its full size, just big enough for it, with the toolbar above it and not over it. Capture the whole screen and press E: the editor fills the screen's height, with the whole capture shown smaller below the toolbar. With two monitors, capture a region on the one that is not the primary and press E: the editor opens centred on that monitor.
- [ ] F-23 (D-124): Click into Notepad, capture a region and press E: the editor opens in front of Notepad with the keyboard focus, and a typed letter does not reach Notepad. Click into Notepad again, capture and press Enter, then click the thumbnail: the editor opens in front, focused. With Notepad focused, open Settings from the tray: Settings comes to the front. No other window flashes in the taskbar.
- [ ] F-30 to F-35: Draw an arrow (A), a line (L), a rectangle (R), an ellipse (O), a pen stroke (P), text (T, typed with an input method too) and a highlight (H). Click a shape to select it, drag a handle, and press Del. Select a shape, press A for the arrow tool, then press Esc: the Select tool is picked and nothing is selected. Press Esc again: the editor stays open. Add a speech bubble (B) and, with the bubble tool still picked, drag the bubble to move it (it stays selected, with a handle on its tail's tip), drag the tail's tip, then click the bubble without moving it: the text box opens. With the arrow tool, click an arrow and drag one of its ends, then press on empty canvas: a new arrow is drawn. With the arrow tool, pick Blue on the strip, then draw a rectangle and a highlight: both are blue.
- [ ] F-41: Add three numbered steps (N): they read 1, 2, 3. Delete the second: the last becomes 2.
- [ ] F-42: Add a speech bubble (B), type in it, and drag its tail's tip. The tail follows and the box stays. Type a sentence of eight or so words into a bubble, then drag its right side inwards: the text wraps onto more lines at the same size and the box grows taller. Copy and paste into Paint: the lines break in the same places. Click the bubble with the bubble tool, add a word, and press Down and Up: the caret moves between the wrapped lines.
- [ ] F-42 (D-126): With the bubble tool (B), drag a box about a third of the capture wide and type a long sentence: the text wraps at the width you dragged. Click once with the bubble tool: a bubble of the usual width appears. Pick the arrow tool and double-click the first bubble: it opens for typing with the caret in it. Do the same to a text box (T): a double-click with any tool reopens it.
- [ ] F-42 (D-128): With the bubble tool, drag a box twice as tall as a line and type one word: the box keeps the height you dragged. Drag its bottom handle down: the box grows taller and the text stays at the top. Drag the bottom handle up past the text: the box stops at the text. Drag a corner: width and height change together. Type lines until they pass the box's bottom: the box grows with them. Copy and paste into Paint: the box is the height shown.
- [ ] F-34, F-42 (D-129): Pick text (T): the strip shows Alignment as a small box of three lines. Press it: a row of three choices opens, each drawn as lines to the left, centre or right. Pick centre and type three lines of different lengths: each line is centred. Pick the bubble tool (B), drag a box three lines tall, type one word and press Esc, then select the bubble (V) and press Alignment: a grid of nine opens. Pick the bottom-right one: the word moves to the box's bottom-right corner. Ctrl+Z: it goes back to the top left. Pick the bubble tool again, set Alignment to centre and drag a new box: its text starts centred. Copy and paste into Paint: the lines sit as in the editor. A press outside the grid closes it.
- [ ] F-43: Add a spotlight (S). Everything outside it dims.
- [ ] F-43 (D-123): Add two spotlights (S): the strip shows Dim with Medium checked, and the dim looks as it did before this setting existed. Pick Dark: the dim deepens around both, in the editor and in Paint. Pick the arrow tool: Dim leaves the strip. Click a spotlight: Dim is back, showing Dark. The "..." menu's Undo row reads "Undo spotlight dim"; Ctrl+Z puts back Medium.
- [ ] F-44: Turn on Border and Shadow under Image frame in the property strip. The exported image gains them, outside the capture's pixels.
- [ ] F-44 highlight and spotlight border (D-134): Pick the highlighter (H): the strip shows Border in a Shape group, and no Shadow. Tick Border, pick Blue and 6 pixels, and highlight a line of text: a blue band rings the highlight just outside it, and the text inside reads as before. Pick the spotlight (S): the strip shows Dim and the Shape group with Border; tick it and draw a spotlight: the band rings it outside, in full colour on the dimmed image. - [ ] F-40 editor buttons (D-133, D-144): In the editor, draw an arrow. Hover the Copy pill: it reads Copy (Ctrl+C). Click it and paste into Paint: the arrow is there. - [ ] F-44 shape shadow: On a light capture at 100%, pick the arrow tool, tick Shadow on the strip and draw an arrow: a soft shadow falls just below it, plainly visible under the line and the head. Draw a rectangle without Shadow ticked: it has none. Select the rectangle and tick Shadow: it gains one, and Ctrl+Z takes it away. ## Settings and policy

- [ ] F-55: Open Settings from the tray. Change the save folder, the format and a hotkey: each takes effect at once with no Save button. Turn off "Show a thumbnail after Copy or Save" and capture: no thumbnail appears. Close Settings with its X: the tray icon stays and PrtSc still opens the overlay. Capture, press E and close the editor with its X: the same.
- [ ] F-56: Turn on Start with Windows, sign out and back in: Cleanshot starts. Turn it off: it no longer does.
- [ ] F-51: In Settings, try these keys in turn as the region hotkey until Cleanshot refuses one: Win+E, Win+R, Win+S. Each time Cleanshot accepts one, set the region hotkey back to PrtSc before trying the next, and note which key was refused. If none is refused, use any key another running program holds. For the refused key, the window says "Another program uses <that key>, so Capture region keeps PrtSc. Press another." and PrtSc still opens the overlay.
- [ ] F-54: Exit Cleanshot. In `cleanshot.toml` (beside the exe, or in `%APPDATA%\Cleanshot`), set `save_format = "gif"` and add a line `colour = "blue"`, then start Cleanshot. One warning says save_format has a value Cleanshot cannot use, so its default applies; nothing is said about `colour`, and a capture saves as PNG. Put the file back as it was.
- [ ] F-59: Capture and press E. Pick Rectangle, set its stroke width to 8 and tick Shadow, pick Text and set its size to 48, then close the editor and exit Cleanshot from the tray. Start Cleanshot, capture and press E: the rectangle draws 8 pixels wide with a shadow and text is 48. Exit, set `stroke_width = 7` under `[looks.rectangle]` in `cleanshot.toml`, and start again: one warning names looks.rectangle.stroke_width, the rectangle draws 3 pixels wide and still has its shadow. Put the file back as it was.
- [ ] F-57: With the Group Policy template from the release (`Cleanshot.admx`, `Cleanshot.adml`), enable "Copy captures only, never save them": Save and Save As are turned off in the overlay and the editor. Enable "Keep the settings window closed": Settings leaves the tray menu.
